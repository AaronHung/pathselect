\begin{abstract}
Whole-slide image (WSI) diagnosis often requires selecting a small set of diagnostically relevant patches from thousands. While continual-learning methods typically focus on preserving predictions or representations, a budgeted system can fail differently: its diagnostic pathway may remain intact while its selector forgets where to look. We introduce \textbf{PathSelect}, a continual evidence-selection framework that preserves and learns where to look across sequential tasks. Crucially, preserving where to look requires maintaining both prior selection behavior and the diagnostic value of selected evidence. PathSelect therefore combines dual-level distillation to retain prior selection behavior with utility preservation that allows evidence to change without degrading diagnostic usefulness. Replay maintains old-task competence, and task-wise updates are consolidated into a single shared selector for task-free inference. \repP{On the four-cohort TCGA benchmark, PathSelect achieves competitive performance}{On the four-task TCGA continual WSI benchmark, PathSelect achieves competitive performance}, with 0.854 class-incremental accuracy and only 0.060 forgetting. Selection-level analyses further show that evidence identity and diagnostic utility are not interchangeable, highlighting the need to preserve how a model acquires evidence.
\end{abstract}

whole-slide images, continual learning, evidence selection, computational pathology, LoRA

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

A gigapixel whole-slide image (WSI) may contain tens of thousands of patches, yet a diagnosis often rests on a few of them. Practical systems therefore select a small evidence set and decide from it; if the selector fails to retrieve the tissue that supports the decision, the decision fails even when the classifier is sound.

Learning new disease cohorts sequentially adds a specific difficulty: it changes the selector's preferences, and the evidence that old cohorts need can be crowded out. Final accuracy shows the resulting drop, but it cannot tell whether the drop originates in evidence selection or in the representation and classifier that conventional continual learning protects.

\repG{}{Existing work addresses the two halves of this problem separately. Multiple-instance learning aggregates patch-level information for slide diagnosis \cite{ilse2018abmil,lu2021clam,shao2021transmil}, and recent methods explicitly learn where to look through tissue-aware selection \cite{huang2026histoselect} or active perception \cite{chen2026adaptivepath}, but they are not designed to preserve that selection behavior across sequential tasks. Continual WSI methods \cite{huang2023conslide,meseguer2024micil,gou2025qpmil,li2025akd,lee2025comel,bui2026mergeslide} instead preserve diagnostic competence through rehearsal, prototypes, attention distillation, or model adaptation, and judge forgetting by downstream accuracy, but do not isolate a budgeted evidence-selection policy as the component that must be retained. The gap is therefore specific: a model may preserve its diagnostic capability while forgetting where to acquire the evidence it needs.}

\repG{We isolate this step. PathSelect freezes the image and text encoders of a vision--language model, which share an embedding space, and the cosine diagnostic rule; only a hierarchical group-to-patch selector, following HistoSelect \cite{huang2026histoselect}, is learned, so any change in outcome traces to the selector. Across tasks the selector is preserved at two levels: distillation keeps its old scoring preferences (\emph{behavioral continuity}), and a utility hinge penalizes any regression in the diagnostic value of the selected evidence while allowing the evidence itself to change (\emph{functional continuity}). Changing the selected patches is therefore not treated as forgetting; losing their usefulness is.}{We address this gap by isolating the selector. \section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

PathSelect isolates continual forgetting at the evidence selector while keeping the diagnostic pathway fixed (Fig.~\ref{fig:arch}). A frozen pathway turns a slide into patch features and, from any set of selected patches, into a diagnosis; only the evidence selector between them is learned. Within a task, the selector learns to spend a budget of $B=8$ patches across tissue groups and then within them, supervised by the diagnosis itself, by a class-text prior, and by the measured usefulness of each candidate patch. \repB{Across tasks, the selector is preserved with three signals drawn from a memory of its own past selections---replay of old samples through reloaded frozen features, distillation of old scores, and a floor on the diagnostic value of the evidence it now selects---while task-local LoRA updates are folded into one shared selector.}{Across tasks, the selector is preserved by replay, distillation of old scores, and a floor on the diagnostic value of its evidence, all drawn from a memory of its own past selections, and task-local LoRA updates are folded into one shared selector.} At evaluation, identity and utility drift are measured separately.

\subsection{From a slide to a diagnosis}
\label{ssec:setup}

Tasks $\tau=1,\ldots,T$ arrive sequentially. \repB{A slide is a set of frozen CONCH patch features $\{x_i\}_{i=1}^{N}$, $x_i\in\mathbb{R}^{512}$.}{A slide is a set of frozen patch features $\{x_i\}_{i=1}^{N}$ from the image encoder.} \repB{Eight fixed tissue prompts are encoded once into $\{p_k\}$; each patch joins the group $G_j$ with $j=\arg\max_k\cos(x_i,p_k)$,}{A fixed vocabulary of $K=8$ tissue-grouping prompts (tumor, stroma, lymphocyte, necrosis, normal epithelium, vessel, adipose, background), distinct from the diagnostic classes, is encoded once by the text encoder into $\{p_k\}_{k=1}^{K}$; each patch joins the group $G_j$ with $j=\arg\max_k\cos(x_i,p_k)$,} and each group is summarized by its mean $g_j$. The group selector $\Fg$ (a two-layer MLP, like $\Fp$) scores the prototypes, $r_j=\Fg(g_j)$, and a largest-remainder allocation converts the softmax over the scores of the non-empty groups with remaining capacity into integer quotas $b_j$ with $\sum_j b_j=B=8$; the patch selector $\Fp$ scores members, $s_i=\Fp(x_i)$, and the top $b_j$ patches of each group form the evidence set $\Psel$. The slide representation is the score-weighted, $\ell_2$-normalized mean $z=\sum_{i\in\Psel}\mathrm{softmax}(s)_i\,x_i\,/\,\bigl\|\sum_{i\in\Psel}\mathrm{softmax}(s)_i\,x_i\bigr\|_2$, and the prediction is the softmax over cosine similarities between $z$ \repB{and the frozen class-text embeddings $\{t_c\}$ of all $C=8$ benchmark classes, fixed from the first task.}{and the frozen class-text embeddings $\{t_c\}$ of the $C=8$ diagnostic subtype classes of the benchmark (Sec.~\ref{sec:exp}), fixed from the first task.} \repB{Inference is exactly this path, with no task identity, memory lookup, or trained head.}{Inference is exactly this path.} $B$ bounds the evidence reaching the head; all patches are still scored. A straight-through factor $\sigma(r)_j/\mathrm{sg}(\sigma(r)_j)$ on the selected members \repL{passes the diagnostic and utility-preservation gradients back to $\Fg$; in the flat ablation variant, $\Fg$ does not enter selection or prediction.}{passes the diagnostic and utility-preservation gradients back to $\Fg$.}



\subsection{Learning to select}
\label{ssec:within}

Within a task, $\L_{\mathrm{ev}}=\L_{\mathrm{diag}}+\beta_s\L_{\mathrm{sem}}+\beta_u\L_{\mathrm{util}}$ provides three kinds of supervision. \repB{\textbf{Outcome}: $\L_{\mathrm{diag}}$ is the cross-entropy (CE) of the frozen head on the selected set; it supervises the joint outcome but assigns credit weakly among candidates.}{The outcome signal $\L_{\mathrm{diag}}$ is the cross-entropy (CE) of the frozen head on the selected set; it supervises the joint outcome but assigns credit weakly among candidates.} \repB{\textbf{Semantic prior}: with $z_{ic}=\cos(x_i,t_c)/T$, a class-text prior}{The semantic signal $\L_{\mathrm{sem}}$ aligns the selector with a class-text prior: with $z_{ic}=\cos(x_i,t_c)/T$, the prior} $p_i\propto 1-H(\mathrm{softmax}_c(z_{ic}))/\log C$ \repB{scores how discriminative each candidate appears, and}{scores how discriminative each candidate appears in the shared image--text embedding space, and} the selector's score distribution over candidates is softly aligned to it with a Kullback--Leibler (KL) term; class-derived information thus enters training only through this prior and the frozen-head losses, never as selector input at inference. \repB{\textbf{Counterfactual utility}: let $E$ be the currently selected set,}{The counterfactual-utility signal $\L_{\mathrm{util}}$ asks which candidate would actually help. Let $E$ be the currently selected set,} summarized by the equal-weight mean of its patches so that every candidate is evaluated under the same weights. \repB{For each unselected candidate $i$ among the top-256 scored patches, the marginal gain}{For each unselected candidate $i$ in a top-scored subset, the marginal gain}
\begin{equation}
u_i
=
\L_{\mathrm{diag}}(E)
-
\L_{\mathrm{diag}}(E\cup\{x_i\})
\label{eq:ui}
\end{equation}
is positive when adding the candidate would reduce the diagnostic loss; it forms a no-gradient teacher, and $\L_{\mathrm{util}}=\mathrm{KL}(\mathrm{softmax}(u)\,\Vert\,\mathrm{softmax}(s))$ trains the patch selector to rank candidates by measured usefulness. The candidate-level $u_i$ is distinct from the value of the whole selected set, $U(\Psel)=\log C-\mathrm{CE}(\Psel,y)$, which is what must be preserved across tasks.

\subsection{Preserving selection across tasks}
\label{ssec:cl}

\repB{After a task is learned, PathSelect stores at most 512 behavioral snapshots in a Selection Memory $\Mem$:}{After a task is learned, PathSelect stores a bounded set of behavioral snapshots in a Selection Memory $\Mem$:} \repL{an internal sample reference, the task tag, group scores $r_{\rm old}$, candidate indices and their patch scores $s_{\rm old}$ and utilities ($\le$256 each), and $U_{\rm old}$.}{an internal sample reference, the task tag, group scores $r_{\rm old}$, candidate indices with their patch scores $s_{\rm old}$ and utilities, and $U_{\rm old}$.} \repB{The memory holds no image or feature tensor (3.1\,MB);}{The memory holds no image or feature tensor;} \repL{during later tasks one snapshot is replayed per step by reloading the slide's frozen features from the training feature store, which replay therefore requires.}{replaying a snapshot reloads the slide's frozen features from the training feature store, which replay therefore requires.}

The continual objective is
\begin{align}
\L_{\mathrm{CL}}
={}&
\lambda_{\mathrm{kd}}
\left[
\mathrm{KL}\bigl(\sigma(r_{\mathrm{old}})\Vert \sigma(r_{\mathrm{new}})\bigr)
+
\mathrm{KL}\bigl(\sigma(s_{\mathrm{old}})\Vert \sigma(s_{\mathrm{new}})\bigr)
\right]
\nonumber\\
&+
\lambda_{\mathrm{eq}}
\max(0,U_{\mathrm{old}}-U_{\mathrm{new}})
+
\lambda_r \L_{\mathrm{diag}}^{\mathrm{rep}},
\label{eq:lcl}
\end{align}
with $\sigma$ the softmax over groups and over candidates.

\repB{\textbf{Behavioral continuity}: dual-level distillation preserves}{Behavioral continuity is enforced by $\L_{\rm KD}$: dual-level distillation preserves} \repB{the old soft policy---group preferences and patch-score distributions---without requiring the hard top-$B$ set to be identical.}{the old soft policy without requiring the hard top-$B$ set to be identical.} \repB{\textbf{Functional continuity}: with $U=\log C-\mathrm{CE}$ the hinge equals}{Functional continuity is encouraged by $\L_{\rm eq}$: with $U=\log C-\mathrm{CE}$ the hinge equals} $\max(0,\mathrm{CE}^{\rm unif}_{\rm new}-\mathrm{CE}^{\rm unif}_{\rm old})$, both terms with equal-weight pooling of the selected set, whereas the replay loss $\L_{\mathrm{diag}}^{\mathrm{rep}}$ uses the head's score-weighted pooling; \repL{the hinge penalizes utility regression on old slides and is inactive otherwise (active in 1.7\%, 3.1\%, and 7.4\% of replay steps at the three continual stages, reverse order, full flat method).}{the hinge penalizes utility regression on old slides and is inactive otherwise.} \repL{An always-on variant that applies the same equal-weight cross-entropy at every replay step is indistinguishable from the gated hinge on five seeds (Sec.~\ref{ssec:ablation}), so the gate is a design choice that makes the utility floor explicit rather than a measured advantage.}{We use this gated form to make the utility floor explicit.} \repB{\textbf{Task competence}: replay applies the ordinary diagnostic loss to replayed old slides.}{Task competence is maintained by replay, which applies the ordinary diagnostic loss to replayed old samples.}

\repG{All terms update the current task's LoRA adapters, attached to the linear layers of $\Fg$ and $\Fp$ while the base weights stay frozen;}{Consolidation uses low-rank adaptation \cite{hu2022lora}, as in its continual variants \cite{liang2024inflora,wu2025sdlora,he2025cllora,chen2026flexem}, only as a fixed-cost parameterization: all terms update the current task's LoRA adapters, attached to the linear layers of $\Fg$ and $\Fp$ while the base weights stay frozen;} at the task boundary each residual is folded in, $W^{(\ell)}_{\tau}=W^{(\ell)}_{\tau-1}+B^{(\ell)}_\tau A^{(\ell)}_\tau$, and the adapters are reset, so model size is fixed and inference uses one shared selector.

\repG{In the reverse order (Table~\ref{tab:sota}, same splits and metrics as the published rows) PathSelect reaches $0.854{\pm}.044$ ACC, $0.925{\pm}.019$ masked ACC, and $0.060{\pm}.041$ forgetting: within one standard deviation of the strongest published result ($0.859$) and above every other continual row. \subsection{What is order-dependent?}
\label{ssec:order}

\repG{The hierarchy's advantage reverses with task order: in the forward order it falls behind the flat selector from the first added task (BRCA, $-0.037$ class-IL, 4/10 folds) and does not recover. Composing independently trained per-task residuals post hoc beat the bare sequential selector in every seed (cf.\ task arithmetic \cite{ilharco2023task}) yet trailed the full method by $13.1$ points (5/5); MergeSlide's orthogonal-projection merging \cite{bui2026mergeslide} did not close that gap ($0.709{\pm}.035$).}{\textbf{The hierarchical substrate is not uniformly beneficial across task orders.} It adds $2.5$ ACC points in the reverse order but costs $3.0$ in the forward order, where it falls behind the flat selector from the first added task (BRCA, $-0.037$ class-IL, 4/10 folds) and does not recover; we therefore treat it as an order-dependent substrate choice, not the source of the robust gain. \textbf{Update consolidation alone is also insufficient to reproduce the full preservation benefit.} Composing independently trained per-task residuals post hoc beats the bare sequential selector in every seed (cf.\ task arithmetic \cite{ilharco2023task}) yet trails the full method by $13.1$ points (5/5), and MergeSlide's orthogonal-projection merging \cite{bui2026mergeslide} does not close that gap ($0.709{\pm}.035$); the benefit comes from consolidation together with preservation, not from merging alone.}

\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}

\repG{The results separate two questions that accuracy alone conflates. Unprotected selectors change where they look and lose the usefulness of what they find; distillation restores the former more than the latter, and the utility term adds the latter without forcing the same patches.}{}

The claims are bounded in three ways. First, four organ-separated, highly separable TCGA tasks, one frozen foundation model, one budget, and known task boundaries \repB{limit generality, and the fold-to-fold spread of our runs and of the reproduced baseline (sd $0.03$--$0.05$) exceeds the gaps between the top methods.}{limit generality; fold-to-fold spread (sd $0.03$--$0.05$) exceeds the gaps between the top methods.} \repB{Second, the hierarchy's advantage and the accuracy increment of distillation plus utility preservation are order-dependent, and the forward-order failure is described only at evaluation time.}{Second, the hierarchy's advantage and part of the preservation gains are order-dependent.} \repB{Third, the head assumes the full class vocabulary as text from the start, replay requires the training feature store, and no privacy guarantee is claimed.}{Third, the head assumes the full class vocabulary as text from the start, and replay requires the training feature store.} \repP{PathSelect is a one-shot evidence-acquisition policy; sequential, stateful acquisition on its task-free selector is left to future work.}{PathSelect is a one-shot acquisition policy; sequential, stateful acquisition is left to future work.}

\section{Conclusion}

Forgetting in budgeted WSI diagnosis can occur in the evidence-selection step alone. PathSelect isolates that step behind a frozen diagnostic pathway, \repP{preserves one shared selector by keeping its old scoring preferences and penalizing loss of evidence utility, and measures identity and utility drift separately;}{preserves one shared selector by keeping its old scoring preferences and penalizing utility loss\delB{, and measures identity and utility drift separately};} \repP{on a public benchmark it recovers most of the unprotected loss}{on the TCGA continual WSI benchmark it recovers most of the unprotected loss}\repB{ and, in the reverse task order, lies within one standard deviation of the strongest published result.}{ and matches the strongest published method within one standard deviation (reverse order).}
